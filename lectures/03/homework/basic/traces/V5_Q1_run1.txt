[answer]
To understand the difference, let's look at what each technique does:

* **LoRA** (Low-Rank Adaptation) is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method where the pretrained model weights are frozen, and weight updates are represented as the product of two small, low-rank matrices. Only these small matrices are trained, which drastically reduces the number of trainable parameters and adapter file sizes.
* **QLoRA** (Quantized LoRA) builds on top of LoRA by quantizing the base-model weights down to a lower precision (such as 4-bit) before applying LoRA. This significantly reduces memory usage, allowing larger models to be fine-tuned on smaller GPUs.

To think about how they relate: LoRA focuses on reducing the *number of trainable parameters*, while QLoRA extends this by also reducing the *memory footprint of the base model* through quantization.
