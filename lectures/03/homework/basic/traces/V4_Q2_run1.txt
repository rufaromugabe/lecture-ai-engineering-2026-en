# question Q2: How are PEFT, LoRA, rank and quantization related to each other?

[answer]
These four concepts are closely linked together as part of the modern toolkit for efficiently adapting large language models:

* **PEFT (Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning):** This is the broad umbrella term for any technique that adapts a pretrained model by training only a tiny fraction of its parameters (or adding a small set of new parameters), rather than updating the entire massive model.
* **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** This is one of the most popular and concrete implementations of a PEFT method. Instead of tweaking every weight in the base model, LoRA freezes the original weights and approximates the weight updates using a pair of smaller matrices.
* **Rank (LoRA $r$):** This is the hyperparameter that controls the inner dimension (size) of those low-rank matrices in LoRA. Think of it as a knob that balances the trade-off between the model's expressive capacity and the number of trainable parameters (and memory required).
* **Quantization:** This is a technique that reduces the memory footprint of a model by representing its weights with fewer bits (e.g., dropping from 16-bit to 4-bit precision). When you combine quantization with LoRA—a technique known as **QLoRA**—you can freeze and compress the base model heavily in low-bit precision while still training your low-rank LoRA adapters effectively on modest hardware.

To think about how they fit together, ask yourself: *If PEFT is the strategy and LoRA is the mechanism, what role do rank and quantization play in scaling that mechanism down to fit on your GPU?*
