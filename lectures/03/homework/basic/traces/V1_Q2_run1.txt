To understand how these concepts fit together, let's look at them from the broadest category down to the specific mechanisms:

1. **PEFT (Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning):** This is the overarching umbrella term. Instead of updating all the billions of parameters in a pretrained model, PEFT methods only train a tiny fraction of them (or add a few new ones), drastically reducing memory and storage requirements.
2. **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** LoRA is a specific, highly popular **PEFT** method. Rather than touching the original model weights, it freezes them and injects small, trainable matrices that approximate the weight updates.
3. **Rank (LoRA $r$):** The **rank** is a hyperparameter that controls the dimensions of those small matrices used in LoRA. Choosing a lower rank means fewer trainable parameters and less memory used, while a higher rank gives the model more capacity to learn complex updates at the cost of more memory.
4. **Quantization:** This is a technique for reducing the memory footprint of a model by representing its weights with fewer bits (e.g., dropping from 16-bit to 4-bit precision). When you combine quantization with LoRA—often called **QLoRA**—you can freeze and compress the base model down to 4 bits to save massive amounts of GPU memory while still training the low-rank adapters effectively.

Think of it as a hierarchy: **PEFT** is the goal, **LoRA** is the strategy to achieve it, **rank** is the dial you turn to control the size of LoRA's matrices, and **quantization** is an orthogonal memory-saving trick that can be layered on top (as in QLoRA) to fit even larger models onto smaller GPUs.
